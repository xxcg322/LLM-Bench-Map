Screen the title and abstract for a study of released LLM evaluation resources.
The unit we collect is a test offered to future researchers, not a finding from
an experiment. This is a selective abstract screen: unresolved evidence does not
pass merely because the full text might contain a benchmark.

Return only p and t.

`p` — what does the abstract establish about the evaluation contribution?
- `B`: it presents a concrete evaluation resource as a contribution for further
  use: test data/tasks, a test environment, or a generator/procedure supplying
  tests. The abstract must support BOTH what the test is and that it is being
  offered as an evaluation resource, not merely used in this paper's experiments.
- `N`: it reports experiments, model comparisons, findings, scoring/auditing
  methods, training resources, surveys, or tools without introducing such a test.
- `U`: a new test resource is plausible, but its evaluation purpose or independent
  contribution is not established by the abstract. Do not upgrade U to B.

Judge the contribution being offered, not whether an experiment could in theory
be repeated. A set of tasks used to demonstrate a method or investigate a finding
does not by itself establish a benchmark release. Likewise, training questions
that could also be used for testing do not establish an evaluation resource.
New metrics or perturbations applied to existing tests are not enough on their
own; a new test suite or test generator must itself be offered as a contribution.

A method and an evaluation resource may be offered together: retain the resource
when the abstract establishes it. Do not require the benchmark to be the main or
only contribution, to have a name/link, or to use particular keywords. Existing
source data may be reused, and full implementation details need not be in the
abstract. These allowances do not replace positive evidence of the test resource.

`t` — what system is directly assessed by the test's scores/comparisons?
- `L`: the intended targets are generative LLMs, LLM-based multimodal models,
  or agents/embodied systems whose reasoning or control core is such a model.
- `N`: the test instead targets non-LLM models, retrieval/classification/detection
  or routing/attack algorithms, benchmark quality, or a mixed LLM/non-LLM target
  population. An LLM merely supplying data, judging outputs, or simulating people
  does not establish an LLM target. Conventional baselines alone do not prove an
  intended mixed population; distinguish baseline roles from intended targets.
- `U`: the intended target cannot be resolved from the abstract. "VLM" or
  "agent" alone does not prove an LLM core. Do not substitute the model discussed
  in the motivation for the entity actually assessed.
A generative LLM judge can itself be the target if its judgments are what the
test evaluates; this differs from using an LLM judge to score some other system.
